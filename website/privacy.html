2. The short version

  • Switch reads the login state only of websites you explicitly grant.
  • Session data is encrypted on your device before it is stored or uploaded. We do not hold your key.
  • Sync is manual by default. Signing in does not upload anything.
  • Optional auto-sync is off by default, limited to granted sites, and requires an unlocked vault.
  • We do not sell data, run advertising analytics, or build a browsing profile.
  • You can export or permanently delete your account from the extension.

3. What Switch accesses on your device

Switch requests host access per site, through your browser's own permission prompt. It does not request access to all websites at install time.

When you explicitly save or restore a session for a granted site, Switch may read:

  • Cookies for that site, including HttpOnly cookies.
  • localStorage and comparable web storage for that site.
  • Session tokens inside those cookies or storage entries.

Switch also reads, locally:

  • The site's hostname, to label the session and bind encryption to it.
  • Coarse browser and operating-system information (for example "Chrome 141 on Windows 10/11") used only as a device label.

Switch does not read passwords you type, form contents, keystrokes, screenshots, page text, sites you have not granted, your full browsing history, other browser profiles, or other extensions.

4. What is stored, and where

4.1 On your device

  • Session snapshots — cookies and storage for granted sites, held in the extension's local storage and encrypted at rest with a key derived from your master password.
  • Vault key — held in chrome.storage.session, cleared when the browser session ends. It is not written to persistent storage.
  • Consent record — the consent version, timestamp, acknowledged categories, and a short history.
  • Local settings and identifiers — a random deviceId, a random per-profile systemId, the granted-host list, and UI preferences.

Signing out clears the vault key and local plaintext caches. It does not delete your local snapshots unless you ask it to; deleting the account does.

5. How encryption works

  • Your master password is processed locally with PBKDF2-SHA-256 to derive separate authentication and encryption material.
  • New accounts use 600,000 iterations; the count is stored per account so older accounts can still sign in and be upgraded over time.
  • Session data is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, bound to its hostname using authenticated additional data, so a server cannot silently move one site's session onto another.
  • The server stores ciphertext only. There is no decryption path server-side.

If you lose your master password, your encrypted sessions cannot be recovered. That is a deliberate consequence of zero-knowledge design.

6. Why we process data

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, we rely on:

  • Contract — to provide the extension and sync service you asked for.
  • Consent — for reading site data and uploading it. You give this per site and can withdraw it; withdrawal deletes saved snapshots and revokes host access.
  • Legitimate interests — keeping the service secure, preventing abuse, and fixing defects, limited to what is necessary and not used for advertising.
  • Legal obligation — where we must retain or disclose something by law.

Where India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act applies, we process personal data on the basis of your consent and the limited "legitimate uses" permitted by that Act. You may withdraw consent at any time.

7. Sharing and third parties

We do not sell personal data and do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. We share only with providers needed to run the service:

  • Database hosting for encrypted envelopes and account records.
  • Email delivery (SMTP) for verification and password-reset messages. The provider sees your address and message content.
  • Infrastructure hosting for the API.
  • Authorised operators, who can see account metadata and masked identifiers. Unmasking is logged. Even an operator cannot decrypt session envelopes.

9. Retention

  • Account and sync data — until you delete your account or the operator removes it. Deleted-session records become tombstones containing no session payload, so deletions propagate to your other devices.
  • Refresh tokens and OTPs — expire automatically through database TTL indexes.
  • Reinstalled devices — reinstalling creates a new random device identifier. If you explicitly merge the previous install, its encrypted session copies are reassigned to the current device and the duplicate device registration is deleted. Merging never decrypts session contents.
  • Feedback — retained until reviewed and then deleted, or for a maximum of [RETENTION PERIOD — TO BE CONFIGURED].
  • Hosting logs — per the hosting configuration in section 4.3.

Account deletion is immediate and irreversible. It removes the account record, encrypted session copies, device registrations, refresh tokens, OTPs, recovery keys and shares. Backups may retain encrypted copies for a limited period before rotation; they contain no plaintext sessions.

10. Security

We use TLS for transport, AES-256-GCM for session data, scrypt for stored authentication verifiers, short-lived access tokens, rotating refresh tokens, per-account storage budgets, and rate limiting. The extension keeps the vault key in session storage and re-seals legacy plaintext snapshots on read.

No system is perfectly secure. Report a suspected vulnerability to security@your-domain.example rather than opening a public issue with exploit details.
