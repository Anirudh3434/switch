Switch

Session Bridge

Move a logged-in session between your own browsers

Sign in once on one browser. Switch saves that site's login state — encrypted — and lets you restore it in another browser, so you don't type your password again.

Nothing is collected in the background. Switch only touches a site after you explicitly grant it access to that site.

What Switch reads, and when

To work, Switch needs access to the login state of the sites you choose. Specifically:

DataWhen it's readWhere it goes
Cookies
including session cookies		 Only when you click Save this session or Restore, on a site you've granted Encrypted on your device
localStorage & site tokens Same — only on granted sites, only when you act Encrypted on your device
  • Access is requested one site at a time, using your browser's own permission prompt.
  • Switch does not request access to all websites when you install it.
  • Your sessions are encrypted with a key derived from your password. Switch's server never holds that key.

What Switch cannot do

We'd rather tell you now than let you find out the hard way.

  • Some sites will still ask you to log in again. Banks, streaming services and Google often bind a session to a device or IP. The cookies restore fine — the site just refuses them. Switch will tell you when that happens.
  • Passkeys can't be moved. They're designed to never leave your device. Nothing can change that.
  • No phone, no Safari yet. Browser restrictions block it. Chromium and Firefox only for now.
  • You are responsible for your password. If you lose it, your saved sessions can't be recovered — not by you, not by us.

Your consent

Please confirm you understand what Switch reads before it can be used.

You can withdraw consent and delete all saved sessions at any time from the extension's settings.

Switch never collects your passwords.
