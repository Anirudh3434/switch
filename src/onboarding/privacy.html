Switch

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026 · Version 2026-10-28

The short version

Switch reads the login state of websites you explicitly grant, encrypts it on your device, and never sees it in a form it can read. Sync is manual by default: signing in does not upload anything. We don't sell data, don't run advertising analytics, and can't recover your sessions if you lose your password.

What Switch accesses

  • Cookies — for sites you grant, read when you click Save this session or Restore. Cookies are how a site knows you're logged in.
  • localStorage and session tokens — for the same sites, at the same moments. Many modern apps store their auth token here instead of a cookie.
  • Coarse device information — browser name, major version, and operating system, used only as a label so you can recognise your own devices. No user-agent string, platform or language is uploaded.

Access is requested per site, through your browser's own permission prompt. Switch does not request access to all websites when installed.

What Switch never does

  • Never reads the passwords you type.
  • Never collects data from sites you haven't granted.
  • Never uploads anything simply because you signed in.
  • Never sends your password or encryption key anywhere.
  • Never uploads sessions unless you explicitly sync them, or you turn on the optional auto-sync setting for sites you granted.
  • Never sells or shares data with advertisers.

How your data is protected

Sessions are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key derived from your password. New accounts use PBKDF2-SHA-256 with 600,000 iterations; the iteration count is stored per account so older accounts can still sign in. Authentication and encryption use separate derivations, so a leaked login hash can't decrypt your sessions.

The ciphertext is bound to the site's hostname with authenticated additional data, so a server cannot silently move one site's session onto another site. Snapshots stored on your device are encrypted at rest too; legacy plaintext snapshots are re-sealed the first time they are read. The vault key lives in session storage and is cleared when the browser session ends or you sign out. This is zero-knowledge: the server cannot read your sessions even if compelled to.

Where data is stored

  • On your device — in the extension's local storage, encrypted. This is the default and works without an account.
  • On a sync server — only if you sign in and choose to sync. The server stores encrypted envelopes plus account and routing metadata (email, KDF salt, encrypted session blobs, host names, versions, timestamps, random device identifiers and coarse device labels). It never stores your password, key, or plaintext cookies.

Auto-sync (off by default)

Auto-sync is an optional setting, disabled by default. Even when enabled, it only runs while you are signed in, your vault is unlocked, consent is current, and the site has been granted. There is no background upload of your whole browser profile.

Sharing with other people

If you choose to hand a session to a teammate, it's re-encrypted to their public key before upload. Only they can open it. We can't. Revoking a share does not retract a session the recipient already imported.

Your control

You can withdraw consent and delete all saved sessions at any time from the extension's settings. Withdrawing consent deletes every saved snapshot, revokes site access and unregisters the content scripts; it cannot be undone. Signing out is local-only and does not delete your account. Account deletion is a separate, explicit action that removes your server-side data as well.

What Switch can't do

Some sites (banks, Google, streaming services) bind sessions to a device or IP and will reject a session moved from another browser. Passkeys cannot be exported by design. We tell you when a restore is rejected rather than retrying silently.
